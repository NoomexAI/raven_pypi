Patients presenting with plasma glucose exceeding 250 mg/dL must be immediately screened for Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA). If the arterial pH falls between 7.00 and 7.24, and serum bicarbonate is between 10.0 and 15.0 mEq/L, the condition is classified as Moderate DKA. If the arterial pH drops below 7.00 or bicarbonate falls below 10.0 mEq/L, it is classified as Severe DKA. Severe cases demand an immediate continuous intravenous insulin infusion protocol at 0.1 units/kg/hr, accompanied by strict hourly fluid-balance and anion gap tracking. Prior to initiating aggressive continuous insulin infusion regimes, the clinical module must verify serum potassium levels. If serum potassium drops below 3.3 mEq/L, insulin administration must be strictly withheld. Under these specific hypokalemic conditions, the medical team must prioritize intravenous potassium replacement therapy (20–30 mEq/hr) until potassium levels successfully cross the safe baseline threshold of 3.3 mEq/L, to prevent acute cardiac arrhythmias.
